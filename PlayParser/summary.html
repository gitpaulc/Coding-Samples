PlayParser

C# console application — Play analysis tool

Purpose

PlayParser ingests raw scene-by-scene text files of plays (currently Shakespeare plays) and produces structured statistics: a dramatis personae, per-character line counts, and a scene-by-scene breakdown of who speaks and who is present. It currently processes three plays: Hamlet, King Henry IV Part 1, and King Henry IV Part 2.

Code Architecture

Two source files, one namespace (PlayParser):

FileRole
Program.cs Utility functions (ToCamelCase, IsUppercase, NumStringToWordString, file I/O helpers) and the Main() entry point that iterates over all plays.
Play.cs Core logic. The Play class holds per-play state (actor set, scene presence map, line-count dictionary) and owns the full processing pipeline.

Processing Pipeline

For each play, Main() calls three methods in sequence:

  1. CopyTrimmedScenes — Reads every scene file from ScenesIn/, runs PreprocessLines to normalize play-specific quirks, calls GetActors on each line to harvest character names from stage directions, then calls RefineActorsCalculation to disambiguate group names (e.g. Soldiers → First Soldier, Second Soldier). Writes cleaned scenes to ScenesOut/ and an Actors.txt dramatis personae.
  2. WriteCounts — Iterates over ScenesOut/ for every known actor. Uses a simple state machine: when a speaker heading (all-caps name) matches the actor, speaking is set to true and lines are counted until another actor’s name appears or an Exit/Exeunt stage direction closes the speech. Populates scenesPresent (which scenes the actor appears in) and lineCounts (line totals per scene).
  3. PrintStatistics — Assembles the final report and writes it to <PlayName>Lines.txt: alphabetical cast list, cast ranked by total lines, per-scene line counts for every actor, and scene-by-scene ensemble lists.

Play-Specific Normalization

The parser hard-codes corrections for each play to handle irregular stage directions:

Hamlet
  • “Enter two Players, King and Queen” → “Enter Player King, Player Queen”
  • “Lord Polonius” and “Prince Fortinbras” normalized to bare surnames
  • “Cornelius Voltimand” split into two separate actors
  • “the Corpse of OPHELIA” treated as an entrance for Ophelia
King Henry IV Part 1
  • “DOUGLAS kills SIR WALTER BLUNT” rewritten as Exit Blunt + Enter Hotspur
  • “Alarum. To the battle.” stage directions decoded to specific character entries
  • “King Henry” expanded to “King Henry IV” to avoid ambiguity with Henry V
King Henry IV Part 2
  • “KING HENRY V” rewritten to “PRINCE HENRY” to prevent confusion with the reigning king
  • Alarum/Excursion stage direction cleanup

Key Design Notes

  • Actor detection relies on the all-caps speaker heading convention of the MIT/Open-Source Shakespeare plain-text format. Lines are counted until the next heading or a stage direction (Exit, Exeunt) interrupts the speech.
  • RefineActorsCalculation disambiguates collective plural names (e.g. Soldiers) by scanning scene files for ordinal-prefixed forms (First Soldier, Second Soldier, …) and replacing the plural entry with its constituent singular actors.
  • Output statistics are sorted descending by total line count using a custom Descending : IComparer<int> passed to SortedDictionary.
  • Scene filenames follow the convention Act.Scene.txt (e.g. 3.2.txt); the numeric act/scene tokens are converted to English words for human-readable output via NumStringToWordString.
